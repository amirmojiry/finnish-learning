# Complete A1.1 curriculum

[نسخه فارسی](A1.1-CURRICULUM.fa.md)

This document is the implementation specification for the complete A1.1 stage. The machine-readable source is [`data/course/a1.1-curriculum.json`](../data/course/a1.1-curriculum.json).

## Scope and level boundary

The curriculum follows the Finnish National Agency for Education A1.1 description: the learner can cope occasionally with a few of the most frequent routine situations, usually with support; understands very short practiced language; and can produce a few short practiced utterances about familiar matters. It also follows the CEFR action-oriented principle that outcomes should be expressed as communicative can-do abilities rather than word-count targets.

A1.1 is therefore intentionally narrow. More independent shopping, transport, appointments, and broader transactional language belong mainly to A1.2/A1.3. A1.1 still includes one highly scripted café order because it is a useful routine micro-situation.

Official references:

- Finnish National Agency for Education: https://www.oph.fi/fi/koulutus-ja-tutkinnot/kehittyvan-kielitaidon-tasojen-kuvausasteikko
- Council of Europe CEFR descriptors: https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-descriptors

## Structure

- **4 sections**
- **10 lessons per section**
- **40 lessons total**
- target of roughly **15 activities per implemented lesson**
- normally **3–6 genuinely new lexical targets** per non-review lesson
- deliberate recycling across later lessons and checkpoints

The four sections are:

1. First contact and survival Finnish
2. Me and people around me
3. Time and a simple day
4. Home, food and the familiar world

## Progression and recycling rules

- A lesson may reference only earlier lessons in `recycle_from`; the final checkpoint may also reference completed section checkpoints.
- Each non-checkpoint lesson should normally introduce no more than 3–6 new lexical targets. Expressions and sentence frames are counted separately because a useful phrase may be learned as a chunk.
- Every section ends in a mixed checkpoint. Section checkpoints do not introduce required new targets.
- New grammar is introduced through a communicative pattern first and then recycled in later contexts. Full paradigms are not required at A1.1.
- A learner should encounter important targets in more than one activity family and in later lessons before the stage is treated as complete.
- Frequency metadata remains source-owned. The curriculum names desired high-frequency targets but never fabricates Parole ranks for curated items or expressions.
- Topic vocabulary is pedagogically selected and may be unranked.

## Four-skill policy

A1.1 tracks listening, reading, speaking and writing, but does not pretend that all four can be assessed equally in the current static browser implementation. Early lessons emphasize listening/recognition and highly controlled production. Speaking-oriented activities may initially be repetition or prompted oral production without automated scoring. Free-form semantic grading and advanced pronunciation scoring remain outside the GitHub Pages-only scope.

## Content-completeness contract

Before an A1.1 lesson can be considered implementation-complete, it must have:

- a stable lesson ID and order;
- English and Persian title and can-do outcome;
- explicit high-frequency and topic streams, even when one is intentionally empty for a checkpoint;
- explicit expressions/sentence frames;
- a grammar or morphology focus, including `recycling only` where no new grammar is introduced;
- explicit `recycle_from` dependencies;
- one or more activity families;
- four-skill coverage flags;
- a measurable completion criterion;
- reviewed learning content and accepted answers when implemented.

The curriculum file is planning metadata. It must not be interpreted as proof that all 40 lessons already exist in the learner-facing application.
